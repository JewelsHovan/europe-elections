2025 Bundestag results, 299 Wahlkreise

The AfD leads the federal polls while Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s CDU/CSU–SPD coalition governs. The latest six-poll simple average here is AfD 27.9%, CDU/CSU 19.5%, Greens 15.3%, SPD 13.6%, Left 11.2% (surveys published 15 September–1 October; sources and field dates below). This is a reversal of the 23 February 2025 result: CDU/CSU 28.5%, AfD 20.8%, SPD 16.4%, Greens 11.6%, Left 8.8%. The polling picture is not a forecast of the next election.

The federal returning officer gives a regular-election window of 26 January to 25 March 2029; no polling day has been fixed. Merz leads the Union; Alice Weidel and Tino Chrupalla co-lead the AfD; Lars Klingbeil and Bärbel Bas lead the SPD; Franziska Brantner and Felix Banaszak lead the Greens; and Ines Schwerdtner co-leads the Left (ARD DeutschlandTrend, Sep 2026; party-leader overview, accessed 1 Oct). In 2025 the FDP won 4.3% and BSW 4.98%, both below the 5% threshold (official final result).

Two votes, not 299 seatsOn the map, the winner of the Erststimme is the leading local candidate. The Zweitstimme is the party vote that determines the Bundestag’s proportional seat distribution. Since the 2023 reform, a constituency plurality is not necessarily a seat unless covered by the party’s second-vote entitlement in that Land; the returning officer explains the coverage rule. The map never labels its constituency counts as mandates.

Battlegrounds

At the six-poll scenario, 107 constituencies have an AfD first-vote lead, up from 46 in 2025; the Union has 111, down from 191. That is a count of modelled pluralities, not Bundestag seats. The model moves AfD +7.1 points and the Union −9.0 points relative to their 2025 national second-vote results (official result; survey average). It holds each party’s local first-vote advantage from 2025 fixed, which is an especially strong assumption in cities.

The Battleground range compares AfD at 26% (Forsa’s latest) with 29.5% (INSA’s latest), moving the Union the other way, with all other parties fixed at the six-poll average; Forsa, 22–28 Sep; INSA, 25–28 Sep. The map counts changes across this range. Select “Battleground range” to see each constituency, and use the list for its numerical margin.

  • Schleswig-Holstein and Lower Saxony: Pinneberg (007) and Nordfriesland – Dithmarschen Nord (002) are Union at 26% but AfD at 29.5% under the model; Salzgitter – Wolfenbüttel (049) moves from an SPD to an AfD lead.
  • Ruhr and Rhineland: Oberhausen – Wesel III (116) and Hamm – Unna II (144) shift from SPD to AfD within the range; Rhein-Erft-Kreis I (090) shifts from Union to AfD.
  • Bavaria and the southwest: Deggendorf (226) and Augsburg-Stadt (251) are near the model’s average tipping point. These are first-vote changes only, not extra seats for the AfD.

The Saxony-Anhalt state election (6 Sep 2026) put the AfD first at roughly 44% (Reuters, 6 Sep). It also led the Mecklenburg-Vorpommern election on 20 Sep, winning 38.2% of the second vote (preliminary, corrected 22 Sep). Berlin voted Left, not AfD: 25.7% Left, 18.8% CDU, 16.3% AfD (Berlin returning officer, preliminary 21 Sep). State elections are not direct measurements of federal support.

Polling

Six latest published surveys, one per pollster, averaged without sample-size or house-effect weighting. The polling average is AfD 27.9, Union 19.5, Greens 15.3, SPD 13.6, Left 11.2; FDP averages 4.0. BSW averages 3.4 among four that report it separately. The AfD has led each of these polls, but the 29 September Forsa survey has it at 26, not 27–29.5 (Forsa). A poll cannot specify who wins in 2029.

Model. For each named party with a 2025 local first-vote candidate, add its current national second-vote polling share minus its 2025 national second-vote share to its local 2025 first-vote percentage; floor at zero, then rescale candidates to sum to 100. A party with no 2025 first-vote candidate stays absent. Smaller candidates are grouped as “Other” and held fixed. For the second-vote projection, add those same national point changes to 2025 local second-vote shares, floor at zero and normalise to 100. Other is the residual polling share; SSW is held at 0.2. The slider moves the AfD share and the Union’s share in opposite directions around the six-poll average, holding other parties fixed. Forsa/INSA preset buttons instead set all their published party numbers; the “Other” residual includes unreported parties. A tie is resolved in the data order (Union, AfD, SPD, Greens, Left, FDP, BSW, SSW, Other). No local poll is used.

Limits. First and second votes move differently. Candidates can retire; parties can nominate candidates where they had none; tactical voting and turnout differ across Wahlkreise. The map’s projected Erststimme leaders are not seats. Proportional seats depend on national and Land second-vote totals, thresholds (including the minority-party exemption and constitutional ground-mandate rule), Sainte-Laguë allocation and second-vote coverage. No seat forecast is calculated here. The 2021 change layer is an actual second-vote change, not an estimate. The six-poll average uses different field dates and no house-effect weighting; about 1–3 point polling uncertainty can move many constituencies across the line.
