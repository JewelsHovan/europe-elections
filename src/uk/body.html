State of the race

Andy Burnham's Labour government remains in office, but a four-poll average (25–29 Sep 2026 fieldwork; sources below) of late September voting intention puts Labour at 26.5%, Reform UK 23.5% and Conservatives 19.8%; More in Common, 26 Sep 2026, and YouGov, 24 Sep 2026, separately project Labour as the largest party in a hung Commons. The next general election has not been called and must take place by 15 Aug 2029 (Commons Library).

Burnham replaced Keir Starmer in summer 2026 (YouGov, 24 Sep). The main party leaders are Labour's Burnham, Reform UK's Nigel Farage, Conservatives' Kemi Badenoch, Liberal Democrats' Ed Davey and Greens' Zack Polanski (Opinium, 26 Sep); John Swinney leads the SNP (Scottish Parliament, May 2026) and Rhun ap Iorwerth leads Plaid Cymru (Senedd, May 2026).

Labour won 411 of 650 seats in July 2024 on 33.7% of the UK vote, against 121 Conservatives, 72 Lib Dems, five Reform MPs on 14.3%, and nine SNP MPs (Commons Library election results, 4 Jul 2024). Reform later won 1,454 English council seats in May 2026 (party tally, 12 May); the BBC projected national share, 9 May 2026, was 26%, not the sometimes-quoted 28% from a different projection. Reform has also faced scrutiny over donations (BBC, Sep 2026), while the September 2026 More in Common MRP has it on 147 Westminster seats, down from 293 in July. These are elections, polls and projections with different electorates, not directly comparable.

Devolved elections on 7 May 2026 in Scotland returned SNP 58 of 129, Labour and Reform 17 each, Greens 15, Conservatives 12 and Lib Dems 10. The Senedd result, 7 May 2026, was Plaid Cymru 43 of 96, Reform 34, Labour nine, Conservatives seven, Greens two and Lib Dems one. These legislatures use different systems and are not added to the Commons map.

Battlegrounds

The battleground layer marks constituencies whose projected winner changes as Reform moves from 21% to 27% of the Great Britain vote, with Labour moving by the same number of points in the opposite direction. It is a range test, not a probability. The More in Common MRP, 26 Sep 2026, independently finds 302 of 631 modelled GB seats won by fewer than five points, and a two-point Labour-to-Reform swing enough to make Reform the largest party.

In Houghton and Sunderland South, Labour won 18,837 votes to Reform’s 11,668 in 2024 (17.9-point lead), yet the uniform-swing illustration moves from Labour to Reform across this six-point poll range. Pontefract, Castleford and Knottingley likewise starts at 17,089 Labour to 10,459 Reform (18.4-point lead) and crosses over in the range. Real 2024 seat examples from the Commons Library candidate results, 4 Jul 2024: Select a seat for its actual votes. Their vote totals provide a baseline, not a reliable prediction: the 26 Sep MRP instead puts Labour's Houghton and Sunderland South within a point of Reform and projects Reform ahead in Doncaster North by one point. The 24 Sep YouGov MRP also flags Conservative–Reform contests in Basildon and Billericay, Hornchurch and Upminster, and Louth and Horncastle.

Sentiment

  • Government satisfaction: Ipsos, 10–15 Sep 2026, reports 18% satisfied and 70% dissatisfied with the government's handling of the country; this is distinct from Burnham's personal rating.
  • Concerns: Ipsos Issues Index, 2–8 Sep, lists immigration at 44%, the economy 29%, inflation 27%; responses are unprompted and can name multiple issues.
  • Direction/trust: Ipsos, 18–23 Sep, finds 61% wrong direction vs 15% right direction. Opinium, 23–25 Sep, scores the government −6 net on rebuilding trust in politics.

Method and sources

Official Commons Library 2024 candidate-level CSV joined by the nine-character constituency code to ONS July 2024 ultra-generalised BUC boundaries (GeoJSON, EPSG:27700). The 2024 GB baseline is computed from valid votes in the 632 GB seats (Labour 34.64%, Conservatives 24.36%, Reform 14.69%, Lib Dems 12.56%, Greens 6.91%). These GB figures differ from UK-wide 2024 headline shares, which include Northern Ireland.

For party p in constituency i, scenario share = max(0, 2024 local party share + (poll GB share − 2024 GB party share)); the winner is the highest adjusted candidate/party share. Independents and minor parties retain their 2024 shares; percentages are not renormalised, since rank, not absolute total, determines the seat. The slider transfers equal percentage points Labour ↔ Reform from the four-poll baseline, holding other shares fixed. SNP/Plaid use an indicative 2%/1% GB anchor (approximately the YouGov 27–28 Sep figures), though their local national polls would be preferable. In Northern Ireland the scenario freezes the entire 2024 field. The Labour change layer uses the official CSV's candidate vote-change field against the notional 2019 result on 2024 boundaries where supplied; missing values are shown as unavailable.

Local support is not uniform: parties did not stand everywhere in 2024, tactical voting, turnout, new parties, devolved-nation swings, defections and by-elections are not modelled. The seat tally is a toy model, not the same as the linked MRPs, and 2024 winner is not necessarily the current MP. Boundaries are heavily simplified for download size; narrow island and urban seats may be difficult to tap, so use search.
