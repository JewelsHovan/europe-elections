State of the race

Spain has no called general election date: Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez says it will be in 2027, with 22 August 2027 the latest legal date (27 Sep 2026). Sánchez's PSOE governs with Sumar and relies on issue-by-issue support from Catalan, Basque and other parties. At the 23 July 2023 election, PP won 137 seats, PSOE 121, Vox 33 and Sumar 31. A September 2026 private-poll average put PP at 32.5%, PSOE 26.2% and Vox 17.6%; PSOE fell 0.9 points from August while Vox rose 0.5. The CIS September survey (14 Sep 2026) instead put PSOE first, 31% to PP's 25.5%, with Vox on 16.6%.

PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo and Vox leader Santiago Abascal seek a majority together. Sánchez leads PSOE; Yolanda Díaz is the leading figure in Sumar. Podemos, ERC, Junts, EH Bildu, PNV, BNG and Canary Coalition can matter to the arithmetic. The September average puts Podemos on 3.3% and Alvise Pérez's Se Acabó la Fiesta (SALF) on 2.0%, neither present as a separate list in 2023.

Battlegrounds

These constituencies change at least one seat when Vox moves from 16% to 20% nationally, holding the other parties at the September private-average targets. They are ranked by the current last-seat D'Hondt quotient gap, not by a forecast of individual candidates. The 3% threshold makes small provinces and new lists especially sensitive; a last-seat change need not change the leading party.

Sentiment

  • Government/PM approval: 62.9% rated the government's management bad or very bad in Sigma Dos / El Mundo (fielded 20–26 Aug 2026). Separately, 26.8% approved Sánchez's management and 67.3% disapproved in SocioMétrica / El Español (28 Sep 2026).
  • Concerns: housing was cited among Spain's three main problems by 37.5%, the economy by 21.6% and immigration by 19.7% in CIS (14 Sep 2026).
  • Trust: 31.5% said Sánchez inspired much or quite a lot of confidence; 19% said that of Feijóo in CIS / EFE (14 Sep 2026). A separate national “right direction” measure was not verified.

Method and sources

Votes and actual provincial seat totals: Election Resources 2023 provincial CSV (reproducing Interior Ministry/JEC reports; 2023 provisional figures) and definitive November 2019 CSV for PP-share change. Geometry: click_that_hood Spain provinces GeoJSON, simplified into SVG; Ceuta and Melilla shown as enlarged tiles. Not an official IGN boundary. Provincial seats reproduce the archived 2023 totals exactly.

The model scales each province's 2023 PP, PSOE, Vox and Sumar votes by scenario national target / 2023 national share. Podemos is seeded proportionally from the Sumar 2023 footprint and SALF from Vox; regional party and other-party votes and 2023 blank ballots stay fixed. D'Hondt awards the district's 2023 number of seats from quotients votes / 1, 2, 3 … to lists with at least 3% of valid ballots including blanks. Ceuta and Melilla elect one seat by plurality. No tactical voting, local candidates, turnout shifts, alliance changes, transfers between regions or fresh regional polling are modelled. Published poll percentages and the model's recalculated national percentages can differ because regional votes and blank ballots are fixed. The live national totals are sums of provincial awards, not proportional national-seat estimates. The PSOE-led column is hypothetical arithmetic, not an endorsement by those parties.
