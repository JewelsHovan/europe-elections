State of the race

Giorgia Meloni's FdI–Lega–Forza Italia–Noi Moderati government faces a close coalition contest, though FdI remains the largest party. A 29 September poll average, published 30 September, puts the traditional right at 41.0%, the broad opposition at 43.6% and Roberto Vannacci's separate Futuro Nazionale at 7.7%. Adding that 7.7 mechanically would be misleading: an Ipsos coalition test reported 28 September instead finds 45.7% for a right alliance including him against 45.2% for the opposition.

The same 29 September average has FdI 26.9% (+0.3 points over the prior two-week average), PD 20.7% (−0.4), M5S 12.7% (+0.4), FN 7.7% (+0.1), Forza Italia 7.4%, AVS 6.3% and Lega 5.6%. The principal leaders are Meloni (FdI), Elly Schlein (PD), Giuseppe Conte (M5S), Antonio Tajani (FI), Matteo Salvini (Lega) and Vannacci (FN); AVS is led by Angelo Bonelli and Nicola Fratoianni. Azione under Carlo Calenda and Italia Viva under Matteo Renzi may or may not cooperate with a broad opposition. The 25 September 2022 Camera election gave the centre-right 43.8% of the list vote and 237 of 400 seats; FdI took 26.0%, PD 19.1%, M5S 15.4% and the then-joint Action–Italia Viva list 7.8%.

No general-election day is fixed. The parliament's five-year term ends in October 2027; the outside constitutional deadline is 22 December 2027, though the education minister said on 25 September 2026 the governing parties expect an October vote. Early dissolution is possible.

Electoral law not decidedAs of the Senate vote on 15 September 2026, the proposed “Stabilicum” would replace the current mixed Rosatellum with proportional seats and a 70-seat Camera / 35-seat Senate bonus for the leading coalition if it wins at least 42% in both houses, subject to seat caps. On 30 September it was still in the Chamber for a final reading. This map does not award seats under either law.

Battlegrounds

In the 2022 regional Camera returns, centre-right led the actual contest in 18 of 20 regions, while M5S led in Campania and an autonomist candidate won Aosta Valley. If PD, the 2022 centre-left allies and M5S are added after the fact, the right's regional lead falls to 3.1 points in Lazio, 4.3 in Marche and 7.3 in Piemonte; the imagined broad side leads by 1.5 in Liguria and 7.0 in Emilia-Romagna. These are list-vote arithmetic, not historical constituency winners.

The southern picture changes more: M5S won 34.6% in Campania (against a 32.2% centre-right coalition), 29.4% in Calabria and 28.2% in Sicily. Under the Ipsos-with-FN preset, the model's right-minus-broad gap is about −3.8 points in Puglia, −0.7 in Calabria and −2.9 in Sicily; all can reverse with a few points' movement or a change in alliance. In 2022 Campania 1's seven single-member Camera seats all went to M5S while all seven in Campania 2 went right. A regional average cannot locate those seats. In the northern industrial belt the right's 2022 aggregated lead was 16.4 in Lombardia and 27.5 in Veneto, much wider than Piemonte's 7.3.

One actual test cuts against reading the regional model as a seat estimate: in the 28 September 2026 Reggio Calabria Camera by-election, the right's Roscioli won 51.02%, the broad candidate Benedetto 36.59% and FN's Giannetta 10.94%, with FN running separately. Turnout, candidates and local alignments matter.

Sentiment

  • Government: YouTrend / Sky TG24, fieldwork 18–22 September, 33% positive and 59% negative, against 36% positive on 4 September. Ipsos, reported 28 September has 40% government satisfaction; different questions and samples.
  • Priorities: YouTrend / Sky TG24, published 4 September found 25% wanted energy-bill support as the next budget's first priority, 24% more healthcare spending, 21% middle-income tax cuts. Government performance on energy was rated negatively by 76%.
  • Direction and trust: the 4 September YouTrend report found 53% saying Italy was going in the wrong direction versus 31% right; 68% had little or no confidence in the government on energy prices. YouTrend, 24 September: 51% of right/FN voters favoured a joint alliance with FN, 31% opposed.

Method and sources

Interior Ministry / Eligendo is the authoritative 2022 Camera source. The rebuild uses cached regional 2022 and 2018 Election Resources results tables (ministry-derived, rounded to 0.1 point); CISE's 26 September 2022 table independently cross-checks the right and centre-left totals. 2022 Aosta single-seat figures are from the Aosta regional election authority's results. Geography is guglielmo/openpolis Italian regional boundaries derived from ISTAT, simplified for this page.

Formula. For each region other than Aosta, projected right share = 2022 right coalition share + (national scenario right − 43.8). Projected broad share = 2022 centre-left + 2022 M5S + (national scenario broad − 41.6). The swing slider adds its points to the right and subtracts them from the broad side. Percentages are vote shares among valid votes and may not sum to 100 because other lists remain unmodelled. The “battleground” colour means |projected right − broad| ≤ 4 points or a winner change under a ±4-point slider swing (an 8-point movement in their two-party gap). Preset values are either the 29 September BiDiMedia 13-poll mean (traditional right / broad camp) or Ipsos coalition tests reported 28 September. FN has no 2022 regional vote: a joined-right scenario spreads its national boost uniformly; it does not locate FN's support.

Limits. 2022 centre-left included +Europa and Civic Commitment; today's “campo largo” may include IV, exclude Azione, or differ entirely. The former Action–IV 2022 vote (7.8% nationally) is kept separate in the map and detail rather than silently reassigned. If alliances change, the comparison changes. These regional shares neither determine the 147 territorial Camera single-member contests used in 2022 nor allocate the 245 proportional and eight overseas seats. In 2022 the right won 121 single-member constituencies and a total 237/400 Camera seats on 43.8% of the domestic vote. A new 42% threshold / bonus law, if enacted, would change the translation from votes to seats. Aosta's separate ballot is never projected. No regional FN polling, voter transfers, turnout adjustment, seat forecast or candidate-level model was verified.
